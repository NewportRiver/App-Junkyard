# App Junkyard — Business Concept

## Vision

Build a low-cost digital salvage yard for abandoned, underused, unfinished, and reusable software, where buyers know exactly what they are getting and rights-holders can recover value from projects they are no longer developing.

## Problem

- Developers abandon prototypes because priorities change, projects fail, or upkeep costs exceed expected returns.
- Small businesses and indie makers repeatedly implement common features from scratch.
- Code marketplaces often lack consistent condition grading, provenance information, security reports, and explicit descriptions of repair effort.

## Value proposition

**For sellers:** Monetize dormant assets without rebuilding a business around them.  
**For buyers:** Buy inspectable, affordable software with documented limitations.  
**For Newport River:** Earn from inventory, consignment commissions, optional repairs, and approved component reassembly.

## Customer segments

1. Indie app/game developers and hobbyists who want a head start.
2. Agencies seeking reusable components or starter projects.
3. Educators and students seeking clearly labeled practice projects.
4. Startup founders validating an idea on a budget.
5. Software rights-holders seeking disposal, consignment, or a modest recovery.

## Revenue models to test

- Direct-sale margin on inventory the company legally owns or can redistribute.
- Commission on third-party listings (later phase).
- Inspection/verification fees for premium seller services (with transparent criteria).
- AI-assisted repair, integration, modernization, and support.
- Curated bundles such as "Starter Dashboard Kit" and "Game Systems Salvage Box."

## Brand and experience

Store metaphor: salvage yard, aisles, shelves, tagged inventory, mechanic bay, rebuilt classics, bargain bin. The aesthetic should be playful and industrial, but key facts—price, license, security status, and compatibility—must remain unambiguous.

Possible slogans to test: "Don't build it. Salvage it."; "Good code deserves a second life."; "One developer's junk is another's launch."

## Operating model

**Recommended launch:** Newport River-owned and specifically authorized inventory, with manual review for every listing. This minimizes early challenges around seller identity, ownership verification, payouts, disputes, fraud, and moderation. Introduce third-party consignment only after repeatable intake and buyer fulfillment are proven.

## Open strategic questions

- Company-owned inventory or full third-party consignment first? (Recommendation: company-owned first.)
- Exclusive source transfer, nonexclusive license, or both?
- Asset storage and authenticated delivery provider?
- Supported platforms and minimum viable build environments?
- Pricing tests and actual buyer demand?
- Human repair service versus automated repair bundles?
